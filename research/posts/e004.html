Playoff-weeks schedule: the spread survives, the forecast does not

Season-long schedule averages out to nothing. Weeks 15 to 17 are only three games and they decide the league -- is playoff-weeks schedule strength worth acting on at draft time?

The spread survives. Three opponents instead of seventeen and schedule luck more than doubles, exactly as the arithmetic says it should. It is still not worth a draft pick, and not because of the spread.

Three games do not average out

E001's finding was really about averaging: one defense spans a full standard deviation by construction, a season of opponents averages down to 0.26, and that collapse was the whole story. 3 opponents cannot collapse as far.

2.3 times wider, and almost exactly what plain averaging predicts with no football in it: 3 draws should span 0.58, 16.5 should span 0.25. Per team rather than per player, which strips out injuries and missed starts, it is 0.56 against 0.23. Divisional games and December scheduling do not clump soft defenses together enough to beat the theory. The window is simply short.

Worth points, fewer than that implies

Points per game in weeks 15-17, regressed on full-season ppg plus the softness of the defenses actually drawn in those weeks: 2684 player-seasons, 8 games in the season, at least 2 of the 3, perfect hindsight on every defense.

A defense a standard deviation softer is worth 0.63 points a game, against E001's 1.18 over a full season. That drop is the short window seen from the other end: a defense's season-long quality correlates only 0.537 against the run and 0.506 against the pass with how soft it actually was in those three weeks. Most of what is knowable about a defense does not turn up in the games that decide anything.

Full-season ppg contains the playoff weeks and absorbs part of the effect. Baselining on weeks 1-14 instead gives 0.81 points per standard deviation over 2301 player-seasons, and 0.0061 of R2 against 0.0037. The pre-2021 window, weeks 14-16, gives 0.66 and 0.0039. Nothing moves.

The whole prizeA soft three-week draw against a hard one is 2.3 points across the run, 9.7% of what an average 7.9-point starter scores over those weeks. With hindsight, before anyone has to predict it.

You cannot see it in August

The two rows that work are the two nobody has at a draft. Opening lines for weeks 15-17 price those games at 0.392 and 0.311, about as well as E001's lines priced a whole season (0.374 and 0.3) — but they post in December. They are here as a ceiling on what a market can know, not as an input.

August holds the top two rows, and they are flat. Last season's defensive numbers land at 0.103 and 0.1, against 0.3 and 0.173 when the target was a whole season. The weeks 1-6 opening lines E001 uses as its August proxy do no better, at 0.097 and 0.141.

The bottom line

The spread comes back, 2.3 times over, and the effect per standard deviation shrinks by about as much, so even the hindsight version lands where the season-long one did. The schedule factor stays at 10% of the composite and stays season-long: a weeks-15-17 version would price a real effect off a signal correlated 0.103 with it, which is worse than not pricing it at all.

Weeks 15-17 are worth thinking about in November, when the lines exist. That is a waiver-wire question, not a draft one.
