We improved our strength-of-schedule model. Then we found out it barely matters.

## We made it better

Today the board uses one input: what each defense gave up last season. Betting markets should beat that, because a line prices a team as it is now. We can read about {weeks_priced} games per team from lines posted before a snap.

::bars caption="correlation with this season's actual points allowed" series="run defense,pass defense" max=0.45
last season|{last_run}|{last_pass}
opening lines|{open_run}|{open_pass}
closing lines|{close_run}|{close_pass}
::

Opening lines are nearly as good as closing lines — the market's August read is most of its final read, which is what makes it usable at a draft. And it helps most against the pass, where last season is nearly useless.

Blending beats either alone, and the optimum is a plateau rather than a knife edge, so the exact weight hardly matters.

## It didn't matter

Then we asked what a perfect schedule number would buy. Not our model — perfect hindsight.

::bars caption="predicting a player's points per game, {n_player_seasons} player-seasons" max=0.7
last year's ppg|{r2_base}
+ perfect schedule|{r2_sched}
::

> The ceiling
> Cheating-level knowledge of every defense improves a player projection by {r2_gain} of R2. Our better model captures a fraction of that.

The reason is structural. Facing a defense one standard deviation softer really is worth {ppg_per_sd} points a game. But players barely differ in the schedules they get: you face seventeen opponents, and averaging seventeen draws collapses the spread.

::bars caption="spread, in standard deviations of defensive quality" max=1.1
one defense|1.0
a whole season's schedule|{sd_schedules_faced}
::

Soft season versus hard season is about {swing_points} points across a year — a few percent of a starter's total.

## What we changed

We took the better model, since it is free now that it is measured. We did not give it more weight. If anything the finding argues the other way: `schedule` is {composite_weight_pct}% of our composite, and it is pricing something worth a few percent of a season.

Season-long schedule averages out. Weeks 15 to 17 do not — three games instead of seventeen, and they decide most leagues. That is where we look next.
