Where ESPN's projections are weakest

ESPN publishes projected carries, yards, targets and receptions. How accurate is each one, and does simply repeating last season already match it anywhere?

ESPN publishes a projected stat line for every player: carries, rushing yards, targets, receptions, receiving yards. Our board consumes one number from it — the fantasy total — and ignores the five forecasts underneath.

Those five are not equally good, and the weakest is not the one you would guess.

Yardage is forecast well. **Receptions are ESPN's weakest stat at 0.656** — which matters more than it looks, because in a PPR league a reception is a point. The thing they predict worst is the thing our scoring rewards most.

Is that just because projection is hard?

The test that matters is not whether ESPN is accurate in the abstract, but whether they beat the dumbest available alternative: assume a player repeats last season. Same players, same rows, so neither side gets an easier sample.

They win everywhereOn 0 of five stats does repeating last season match ESPN. The margin runs from 0.056 on receiving yards to 0.093 on carries.

So ESPN is genuinely doing work. But look at the size of it: their edge over a baseline with no model at all is between 0.056 and 0.093. They are better than doing nothing, and not by a great deal.

Where they earn it most is **carries**, which is where a projection has to know something a stat sheet cannot tell you — who the team signed, who is hurt, how a backfield will be split. And they earn it completely on rookies, where the baseline has nothing to say at all: ESPN still forecasts a rookie's receiving yards at 0.744 across 446 of them.

What this means for building our own

Two things, pointing in different directions.

Beating ESPN is not a formality. They are ahead of the naive baseline on every stat, they handle rookies, and any model of ours has to clear a bar we now know the height of.

But the bar is not high, and it is lowest exactly where PPR scoring is decided. 0.056 to 0.093 above "repeat last year" is a target a genuinely informed model should reach — and receptions, at 0.656, is the softest number on the board.

That is where we start.

  • Season 2023 is excluded: ESPN returns 89 usable projections for it against roughly 490 in a normal year, and a season that thin would speak for players it never covered.
  • These are ESPN's PRESEASON projections, confirmed rather than assumed: against realised outcomes they correlate 0.66-0.70 on fantasy points at 70-117% mean absolute percentage error. A projection contaminated by in-season results would sit above 0.95.
  • Rows are filtered to an actual of at least 20, because a projected zero carries for a receiver is a position fact rather than a forecast, and including those inflates every correlation.
  • The baseline is last season's raw total with no adjustment for games missed, age or a change of team. It is a floor to clear, not a rival model, and a real projection should beat it comfortably.
  • ESPN's id joins via nflverse's id table, not this database's `sleeper_ids`, which covers only 22-39% of recent seasons.
