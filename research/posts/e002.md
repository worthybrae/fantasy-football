Tested against running back fantasy points, an offensive line rating does nothing. We wrote that down as "the line doesn't matter." Tested against yards per carry, the same rating on the same teams is one of the clearer effects in this project.

## The rating is a price, not a grade

There are no line grades in this database — no pressure rate, no run-block win rate, no PFF. There is what teams paid. Take the five linemen who took the most snaps for a team that season and average their contracts as a share of that year's cap: the market's own estimate of a line, already adjusted for the cap going up.

::curve keys="coverage_curve" series="priced" caption="share of each season's five starters with a contract on file" xlabel="{first_season} ------ {n_team_seasons} team-seasons ------ {last_season}"
::

Between {coverage_min}% and {coverage_max}% of starters price out, so a rating is five real contracts rather than three and a shrug. It names the right teams, too: {top_lines} paid the most in {recent_season}, {bottom_lines} the least.

## Against fantasy points: nothing

Predict a back's points per game from last year's, then add what his line costs.

::bars caption="predicting points per game, {n_rb_seasons} player-seasons"
last year's ppg|{r2_ppg_base}
+ line spending|{r2_ppg_line}
::

R2 moves {r2_ppg_gain}, and a standard deviation of spending buys {ppg_per_sd} points a game. That was not a bad measurement. It was a good measurement of the wrong thing: fantasy points are carries and touchdowns, and a line does not hand out carries.

## Against yards per carry: something

Same rating, same shape of test, one word different in the outcome.

::bars caption="predicting team yards per carry, {n_team_ypc} team-seasons"
last year's team ypc|{r2_ypc_base}
+ line spending|{r2_ypc_line}
::

It turns up again where nothing is shared but the rating. {n_moves} times a back with at least {min_carries} carries in each of two seasons changed teams, and so changed lines wholesale. The change in what his line cost correlates {r_move} with the change in his yards per carry, and {r_move_strict} among the {n_moves_strict} who carried it {strict_carries} times in both years.

::bars caption="yards per carry, per standard deviation of line spending"
team, mostly the same line|{ypc_per_sd}
back who changed lines|{ypc_per_sd_move}
::

The mover's number is the bigger one, as it should be. Team spending correlates {line_autocorr} with its own spending a year earlier, so last year's yards per carry is already half a line measurement and absorbs part of the effect. A back who signs elsewhere gets the whole swing at once.

> Where it lands
> A median starter takes {starter_carries} carries. A standard deviation of line quality is worth {season_points_per_sd} to {season_points_per_sd_move} fantasy points to him across an entire season.

## What we changed

Not the weight. What changed is what the input may claim: it prices efficiency, so judge it on efficiency. A projection denominated in points will read it as noise however good the rating gets.

And if the line moves yards and not points, then points are volume — a coaching decision rather than a blocking one. That is the thing worth predicting next.
